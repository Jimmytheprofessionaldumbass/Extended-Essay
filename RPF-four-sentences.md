## Slot 3 (~35 words) — what rejecting the broad question taught you

Your sentence 4 already says the broad question "would have resulted in a paper that is essentially a
compilation of all possible factors". The judgement is the next step:

> Rejecting that question taught me that a research question has to be ______ , because ______ .

Two candidate directions, pick whichever is true for you:

- **"…has to be answerable with the equipment I have, because an interesting question I cannot measure
  produces a compilation rather than a finding."**
- **"…has to name a mechanism, not a topic, because 'what factors affect X' invites a list while 'does
  factor Y change Z' invites an answer."**

If the supervisor said something at the viva about scope, put that sentence here instead — the guide calls
the viva the main source for this statement.

## Slot 4 (~45 words) — whose decision was the design flaw

You already wrote the honest version, buried inside the bearing-screw digression:

> "I should have looked a bit more into the experiment and adapted my experiment into it"

Frame:

> The sensor failure was not bad luck; it was ______ . I chose the design before I had read ______ , and
> the cost was ______ .

The point that earns the mark: **naming it as your decision rather than the equipment's failure.** Do not
say "the sensor was inadequate" — say what you did not do before choosing it.

## Slot 8 (~40 words) — the finger as a variable

You discovered the finger is not a constant material, which is a design lesson rather than a result.

Frame:

> Since then I treat ______ as a variable rather than a constant, which means in a future experiment I
> would ______ before ______ .

The specific thing to name: what you would now hold fixed or measure separately, that you previously
assumed away. If the honest answer is "clean the finger to a protocol and record what I used", say that.

## Slot 10 (~90 words) — the transfer statement

This is the only part of the statement that cannot be inferred from the essay, and the checker will fail
without it. Three sentences:

> 1. The thing I will do differently in the next piece of research I am handed is ______ .
> 2. This project is why: I began with ______ and learned ______ .
> 3. What that changes about how I work is ______ .

The strongest honest answer is already in your history — you started with a question too broad to answer
and learned to design around what can be measured. If you write that, the transfer is *demonstrated* rather
than claimed, which is what criterion E rewards.

**Avoid:** "I learned time management" or "I learned to be organised". The criterion asks for specific
examples, and generic lessons read as filler.
