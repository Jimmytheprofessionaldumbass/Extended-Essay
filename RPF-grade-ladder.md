# What the EE grade descriptors actually require of the reflection

Verified 2026-10-29 from `Sources/extended-essay-first-assessment-2027-guide-sbs.pdf`, pp. 115–117
(the grade descriptors themselves, not a summary). The reflection appears in **every** band, so it is the
one part of the essay where the difference between grades is stated in plain words.

## The ladder, verbatim

| Band | Descriptor for reflection |
|---|---|
| **A** | "consistently **evaluative** and includes **specific examples**" · "**consistent evidence** of the student's growth and **transfer of learning**" |
| **B** | "**partially** evaluative and includes specific examples" · "**often** shows evidence of growth and transfer" |
| **C** | "**descriptive** and includes some specific examples" · "**sometimes** shows evidence of growth and transfer" |
| **D** | "**descriptive**" · "growth is limited to a **description of the process** and there is **no evidence of transfer**" |
| **E** | "**very limited**, with **no evidence** of transfer of learning" |

## What this means concretely

Three words move the grade, and only three:

1. **Descriptive → evaluative.** Descriptive = what happened. Evaluative = what it meant. A C is a
   well-written description; a B is a description with judgements attached; an A is *consistently* judged,
   with no purely descriptive paragraph left.
2. **Some examples → specific examples.** Not "my experiment had problems" but "the force sensor locked at a
   constant reading when the applied force was held steady, which nullified that trial's data".
3. **Growth and transfer.** Growth = something changed in how he works. Transfer = it will apply
   somewhere else. **D is the band with no transfer at all**, which is precisely what his current 649-word
   draft does — it is a process description.

The honest reading: his draft is not badly written, it is at the wrong *kind* of writing for the criterion.
Six evaluative sentences and one transfer statement move it from D to B. Consistency moves it from B to A.

## The one sentence that separates B from A

The descriptors say **"consistently" evaluative (A) vs "partially" evaluative (B)**. In practice that means:
does *every* paragraph end on a judgement, or do some just stop after describing? The test is mechanical —
read each paragraph's last sentence and ask whether it says something about him as a learner.
